Building A Plot

When it comes to plot, we need to make sure that something is always happening, or else the story will stall and get boring. An unsual way to do this is to keep adding twists and turns to the story, though this doesn't always work as it can just confuse the reader and unintentially make the plot more complex if not done properly.

The importance of keeping the plot interesting has lead writers today to create one continous plot (and sometimes side plots) that lasts through the whole story (although this isn't always the case, as there are many stories out there that don't stick to the same plot/direction for the whole time, it is done quite frequently). A typical plot usually goes through a cycle of 5 main parts:

  1. The Inciting Incident: The first major event in the story that changes something big and creates a goal/motive for the main cast, usually a shorter, action-packed, and epic moment.
  2. The Rising Action: A longer period of time that builds up suspence and hype for one big upcoming event/moment, starting shortly after the inciting incident.
  3. The Climax: A massive, important moment (like a battle, argument, or death) that the story so far had been leading up to. Typically even more epic than the inciting incident.
  4. The Falling Action: A shorter period of time that acts as the clean-up of the climax and the set-up of the last part. Usually much shorter than the rising action.
  5. The Resolution: The final ending of the story, wrapping up everything that has happened so far (unless the ending is a cliffhanger!).
